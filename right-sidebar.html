J&J Internship Projects

Test PCB for Signal Amplification and Noise reduction

It involves usage of three different in-amp circuits. This you can see as the three different lines (vertical) of circuits, beginning with connectors and ending with connectors. They take input from torque cell sensors attached to robot motor arm drivers, the signal then goes through tvs diodes and then an instrumentation amplifier. It finally goes through another connector and goes to the larger board. A potentiometer is added to adjust the gain for the instrumentation amplifier and noise reduction is kept minimum.

Two of the circuits involve usage of off the shelf in-amps and one is my own custom design 3 op-amp in-amp. Involved usage of Altium Designer for layout, routing and schematic. Used for signal amplification which it improved by 40%. Involved system validation and failure analysis. Finished entire process from ideation to fully functional board in 6 weeks.

Testing the PCB

After having my PCB manufactured I ran a series of tests to check its functionality which involved checking the right voltages through the chosen testpads in my design, generating wavefunctions through the signal generator and then seeing its amplitude before and after the in-amp for different potentiometer settings. Through my testing I found out that only 2 out of 3 of the circuits worked functionally.

The reason for this was that because of the custom in-amp design which I had, one of the parts involved in it did not have a proper design on Altium. This did not allow that circuit to be pwoered, causing an entire row of circuit to be lost. This was circumnavigated by replacing that part and recreating the trace via actual wires to bypass that component. However, this issue took a while to figure out and taught me that for next PCB design not ony should I check parts more in detail before sending out the PDB for manufacturing but also add LEDs to each circuit which would give a visual indication of whether a circuit is being properly powered or not. I also learnt of TVS diodes and their importance for ESD.

Modular bare metal firmware driver project

Project involved use of STM32 nucleo boards and SparkFun buttons to create custom buttons which could be used with surgical tools to enhance their functionality and reduce demo time (which it did to 20%). Involved complete creation of function libraries and a modular code from scratch to enable functionality of the SparkFun buttons with the STM32 (as they are Arduino based but also to allow customization according to company needs).
